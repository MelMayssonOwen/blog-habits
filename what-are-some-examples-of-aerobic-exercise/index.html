What Are Some Examples of Aerobic Exercise?

Published 2026-11-15

TL;DR: Aerobic exercise is movement that raises your heart rate and makes you breathe faster. Common examples include brisk walking, dancing, cycling, swimming, running, stair walking, sports, skipping, aerobics, gymnastics, and martial arts. The NHS talk test can help you judge whether an activity is moderate or vigorous, and a simple if-then plan can make the habit easier to repeat.

What counts as aerobic exercise?

Aerobic exercise is physical activity that boosts your heart rate and speeds up your breathing. In NHS terms, it is exercise that makes you breathe more heavily and feel warmer, while still leaving you able to talk at a moderate intensity.

Beginner-friendly examples include brisk walking, dancing, cycling and water aerobics. The main idea is simple: if your heart rate and breathing increase, you are likely doing aerobic exercise.

Common examples of aerobic exercise

The NHS groups aerobic activity by intensity, which helps because the same activity can feel very different at different paces.

Easy everyday aerobic options

These practical choices fit into ordinary life:

  • brisk walking
  • dancing
  • riding a bike
  • water aerobics
  • hiking
  • rollerblading

More intense aerobic options

When you push harder, these activities become more demanding:

  • running
  • swimming
  • fast or hill cycling
  • stair walking
  • team sports such as football, rugby, netball and hockey
  • skipping
  • aerobics
  • gymnastics
  • martial arts

How to tell whether an activity is aerobic enough

Use the NHS talk test to judge intensity without gadgets. At moderate intensity you can talk but not sing. At vigorous intensity you can only say a few words before pausing for breath. If you can chat comfortably through the whole session it may be too easy; if you cannot speak at all it is likely vigorous.

A beginner worked example: turning one normal day into aerobic movement

Choose one realistic, repeatable change and make it part of your routine. Example plan:

  • Pick a single cue you already have in your day, such as finishing work or lunch.
  • Swap one short car trip or elevator ride for walking or the stairs.
  • Add a brief brisk walk after a meal or as part of your commute.

Practical if-then example you can use as written: "If I finish lunch, then I will take a brisk walk." That links a familiar moment to a simple action and makes it easier to follow through.

Make it stick with an if-then plan

Implementation intentions are simple if-then plans that map a situation to an action. Forming a plan like "If X happens, then I will do Y" reduces the chance of forgetting or getting derailed.

Tips to keep the habit working

  • Start small so the action is easy to repeat.
  • Attach the action to a specific cue you encounter daily.
  • Repeat the same cue-action pairing until it feels automatic.
  • If you miss a day, return to the same cue at the next opportunity.
